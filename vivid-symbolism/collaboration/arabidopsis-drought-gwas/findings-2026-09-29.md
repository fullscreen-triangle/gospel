# First pass on the WCS417 drought-rescue GWAS data

Data: `vivid-symbolism/public/gwas/GWAS Kundai.xlsx` (sheets *Biomass data*,
*easyGWAS input file*, *easyGWAS output*, *Accessions*, *Table 1*, *Table 2*),
`Presentation.pptx`, and Wintermans et al. 2016 (*Plant Mol Biol*,
s11103-016-0442-2). Scripts in `analysis/`, numbers in
`analysis/respind_real.json`. Everything below is reproducible from those.

## 1. What is in the workbook

- **Per-plant raw data exist for one cell only:** WCS417 × drought.
  1,841 plants: 247 accessions × 7 plants. Three lines were grown repeatedly:
  Col-0 (6909) 98 plants in 14 blocks, and 7394, 9807, 9874 with 14 plants each.
- **The other three cells** (mock × drought, mock × non-stress, WCS417 ×
  non-stress) appear only as pre-computed per-accession averages and medians
  inside the derived traits. Per-plant values for them are not in the file.
- **Primary root length** is filled for 1,042 of 1,841 plants.
- **Table 1 is the drought condition.** Its mock and WCS417 columns match the
  recovered mock-drought and WCS417-drought means (r = 0.96, 0.97).

## 2. The trait formulas, recovered exactly

Write W = WCS417 drought, M_D = mock drought, M_N = mock non-stress. These hold
for all 247 accessions to rounding error (~1e-10):

| Trait in sheet | Formula | Involves bacterium? |
|---|---|---|
| AVG/Median Shoot FW | W | yes (plant size under WCS417) |
| Gain in … Drought | W − M_D | yes |
| Percent increase in … | W / M_D − 1 | yes |
| Percent Rescue … | (W − M_D) / (M_N − M_D) | yes |
| Percent Loss … under drought | 1 − M_D / M_N | **no — mock only** |
| Gain in … Non Stress | W_N − M_N **or** W − W_N (the data can't tell these apart) | ambiguous |

Consequences:

- **Table 2 is mislabelled.** Its "Drought Tolerance" columns equal the
  "Percent Loss" column (239 of 240 identical). They are fractional *loss*:
  a higher value means *less* tolerant. Median shoot loss is 0.78.
- **Three candidate genes are not about WCS417 at all.** MORN4, AT2G07213 and
  TPS21 came from the Loss metric, which uses only mock plants. They are
  drought-response loci, not rescue loci.
- **"Non-rescuer" is a threshold, not a loss of rescue.** Every accession has
  positive gain (minimum 0.73 mg shoot, 0.27 mg root). The binary label splits
  at about 6–7.75 mg shoot gain (91 of 243 accessions labelled non-rescuer) and
  about 2.8 mg root gain (57 of 243). This matches the voice note: rescue varies
  in degree, never lost.
- **Four accessions don't match the % increase formula** (6909, 7394, 9807,
  9874). These are exactly the repeatedly grown lines, so their summaries were
  computed from a different subset of plants than their other traits.

## 3. Response independence on real phenotype data

Gain, % increase and rescue are three reasonable ways to define "the response of
accession *x* to WCS417 under drought". All three use the same cell means. This
is the paper's response-independence test (the construction in section
"The experiment that decides response independence"), here on real phenotypes
instead of a solved pathway.

**Agreement between the three constructions** (Spearman ρ across 247 accessions;
top-20% = 49 accessions):

| | Shoot ρ | Root ρ | Shoot top-20% Jaccard | Root top-20% Jaccard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gain ~ increase | 0.78 | 0.59 | 0.44 | 0.18 |
| gain ~ rescue | 0.78 | 0.61 | 0.42 | 0.26 |
| increase ~ rescue | 0.67 | 0.41 | 0.32 | 0.17 |
| top-20% under all three | | | 16 / 49 | 7 / 49 |

**Stability under plant-level resampling** (500 bootstrap draws of the plants;
mock cells perturbed with the same CV, 0.12):

| | rank ρ vs original | top-20% members retained |
|---|---|---|
| gain | 0.95 | 85% |
| increase | 0.97 | 89% |
| rescue | 0.92 | 79% |

**Reading:** plant-to-plant noise barely moves the rankings. Switching formula
moves them a lot. So the disagreement between trait lists comes from the
construction, not from sampling. In the paper's terms, response independence
fails on this dataset too: which accessions count as strong rescuers depends
on which admissible response you construct, much more than on noise.

**Why they disagree: each construction carries a different nuisance.**

| | ρ with W (plant size) | ρ with mock-only drought loss |
|---|---|---|
| gain | 0.61 | 0.45 |
| increase | 0.06 | **0.80** |
| rescue | 0.40 | 0.15 |

- **% increase** divides by M_D, so drought-sensitive accessions (small M_D)
  get inflated ratios. It ranks accessions almost like the mock-only drought
  loss (ρ = 0.80). **12 of the 28 candidate genes come from increase traits.**
  Many of them may be drought-sensitivity loci in disguise.
- **Gain** in mg tracks plant size.
- **Rescue**, normalised by each accession's own drought loss, is the least
  contaminated by either nuisance. It is also the least stable under
  resampling, because it is a ratio of differences.

## 4. The candidate gene list

28 unique genes over 40 rows:

| Trait family | Genes |
|---|---|
| Relative increase | 12 |
| Rescue | 9 |
| Plant size (W only) | 3 |
| Mock-only drought loss | 3 |
| Absolute gain | 1 |

- **7 genes appear under more than one metric, but none under more than one
  trait family.** Every "agreement" is between near-duplicates: AVG vs median of
  the same trait, or shoot vs total (total includes shoot).
- So in this list, **no locus holds up across different definitions of
  rescue.**
- The Manhattan plots use a threshold of about −log10 p ≈ 8, and most hits are
  single SNPs just above it. Minor-allele frequency (MAF) filtering, and whether
  kinship was corrected for, need checking.

## 5. Batch

Col-0 was grown in 14 blocks of 7 plants spread across the screen. That makes it
a within-run batch control:

| | Shoot | Root |
|---|---|---|
| Between-block SD / between-accession SD | 0.43 | 0.53 |
| Share of accession variance batch alone could produce | ~19% | ~28% |
| Where the Col-0 block means fall among accessions | 52nd–91st percentile | 70th–98th percentile |

These blocks are only WCS417 × drought. Batch in the mock cells is unknown. The
Col-0 blocks can be used as a per-batch covariate if we know which accessions
were grown in which block.

## 6. What this means for the collaboration

1. **The rescue phenotype needs one fixed construction before any further
   GWAS**, chosen for which nuisance it removes, not for which one gives peaks.
   The cleanest option is a single model on per-plant data for all four cells,
   with the WCS417 × drought interaction as the phenotype, plus batch and
   kinship. That needs the per-plant mock and non-stress data.
2. **Candidates can be triaged now:**
   - **Mock-only (drop from the rescue story):** MORN4, AT2G07213, TPS21.
   - **Increase-only (at risk of being drought-sensitivity loci):** 12 genes.
   - **Rescue-derived:** the 9 most interpretable candidates for the mutant
     screen.
3. **The iron result in the RNA-seq note needs the same check.** "Iron-uptake
   mutants show higher rescue" could come from the mutants' lower mock baseline
   if the metric is % increase, since smaller M_D inflates W/M_D. The absolute
   gain and the (W − M_D)/(M_N − M_D) rescue should both go up too.
4. **This dataset is a better test case for the paper than the four pathways**:
   the failure of response independence here is measured on real phenotypes,
   with a noise floor to compare it against.
